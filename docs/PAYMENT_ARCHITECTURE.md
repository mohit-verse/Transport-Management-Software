# Payment Architecture

## 1. Purpose

The Payment Module is the authoritative financial transaction system of the SRL application.

It records all actual incoming and outgoing business money movements.

The Payment Module is also the sole source for:

- Payment history.
- Payment allocations.
- FIFO allocation.
- Party/Company credits.
- Payment-based financial reports.
- Payment-based business P&L.
- Financial-year payment records.

Trip and Bill records may contain financial calculations and references to payments, but they must not become separate payment ledgers.

---

# 2. Core Principle

The system must maintain a strict separation between:

### Financial Obligation

Created by operational/business records such as:

- Trip receivable.
- Vehicle Owner payable.
- Bill amount.
- TDS.
- Deductions.
- Own Fleet expense obligation.

### Actual Money Movement

Recorded only through the Payment Module.

Examples:

- Company pays SRL.
- Market Party pays SRL.
- SRL pays Vehicle Owner.
- SRL records an Own Fleet expense payment.
- SRL receives another business receipt.
- SRL makes another business payment.

The Payment Module records the actual transaction.

---

# 3. Payment Types

Every Payment must have one of two primary types:

- Incoming
- Outgoing

## Incoming

Money received by SRL.

Possible categories:

- Company Payment
- Market Party Payment
- Other Business Receipt

## Outgoing

Money paid by SRL.

Possible categories:

- Vehicle Owner Payment
- Own Fleet Expense
- Other Business Payment

---

# 4. Payment Record

Every Payment must contain:

- Payment ID
- Payment Date
- Payment Type
- Entity
- Category
- Amount
- Payment Mode
- Status
- Created By
- Created Date/Time
- Last Edited By
- Last Edited Date/Time

## Payment ID

Payment ID is automatically generated by the system.

Users must not manually assign Payment IDs.

---

# 5. Payment Date

The Payment Date represents the actual date on which the money movement occurred.

The system should:

- Default the date to today.
- Allow the authorized user to change it.
- Preserve the actual selected date in the Payment record.

Financial reporting must use the Payment Date.

---

# 6. Payment Modes

The system supports:

- UPI
- Bank Transfer
- Cash

The system must not assume that Cash is unavailable simply because it is not normally used.

Payment Mode must remain part of the Payment record and available for filtering/reporting.

---

# 7. Payment Status

Allowed Payment statuses:

- Active
- Reversed

## Active

The payment is valid and contributes to financial calculations.

## Reversed

The payment has been cancelled through the authorized reversal workflow.

A reversed payment:

- Remains in history.
- Does not contribute to financial totals.
- Does not contribute to P&L.
- Does not satisfy outstanding obligations.
- Has its allocations reversed.
- Can affect trip settlement status.
- Can affect bill payment status.
- Can affect Party/Company credit.

---

# 8. Payment Creation

Payments must be created through the Payment Module.

The system must not create an independent payment record from:

- Trip editing.
- Bill generation.
- Party editing.
- Vehicle Owner editing.

Those modules may initiate a payment workflow, but the resulting actual money movement must be stored in the Payment Module.

---

# 9. Incoming Payments

Incoming payments represent money received by SRL.

They may originate from:

1. Company.
2. Market Party.
3. Other Business Receipt.

---

# 10. Company Payment

A Company Payment is an incoming payment from a Company.

It may be allocated to:

- A specific Bill.
- A specific Trip.
- Multiple outstanding receivables through FIFO.

Company payments can generate Company credit when the amount received exceeds the applicable outstanding receivables.

---

# 11. Market Party Payment

A Market Party Payment is an incoming payment from a Market Party.

It may be allocated to:

- A specific Trip.
- Multiple outstanding receivables through FIFO.

Market Party payments can generate Party credit when the amount received exceeds the applicable outstanding receivables.

---

# 12. Other Business Receipt

An Other Business Receipt is an incoming payment that is not a Company Payment or Market Party Payment.

Required information:

- Category/Name
- Amount
- Date
- Payment Mode
- Mandatory Remark

Optional:

- Attachment

Other Business Receipts are standalone financial records.

They are not allocated against Trip or Bill receivables unless a future approved workflow explicitly introduces such behavior.

---

# 13. Outgoing Payments

Outgoing payments represent money paid by SRL.

They may originate from:

1. Vehicle Owner Payment.
2. Own Fleet Expense.
3. Other Business Payment.

---

# 14. Vehicle Owner Payment

A Vehicle Owner Payment is an outgoing payment against a Market Vehicle Trip.

It may be associated with:

- Vehicle Owner.
- Trip.
- Applicable payable obligation.

The Payment Module records the actual amount paid.

The Trip records the calculated payable and references the actual Payment.

---

# 15. Own Fleet Expense Payment

Own Fleet Expenses are outgoing business payments associated with Own Fleet operations.

Supported expense categories:

- Diesel
- FASTag
- Loading
- Unloading
- Border
- Other

Actual money movement must be recorded through the Payment Module.

The Trip may show the expense and related payment information but must not create a second payment ledger.

---

# 16. Other Business Payment

An Other Business Payment is an outgoing payment that does not belong to Vehicle Owner payable or Own Fleet trip expenses.

Required information:

- Category/Name
- Amount
- Date
- Payment Mode
- Mandatory Remark

Optional:

- Attachment

Other Business Payments reduce overall business P&L.

---

# 17. Entity Selection

Where a Payment is associated with a master entity, the entity must be selected from the corresponding master.

The system must not use unrestricted free-text entity names when an appropriate master exists.

Examples:

- Company Payment → Party/Company Master.
- Market Party Payment → Party/Company Master.
- Vehicle Owner Payment → Vehicle Owner Master.

Other Business Payment/Receipt categories may use the category/name structure approved for those records.

---

# 18. Payment Allocation

Payment Allocation determines which financial obligation an actual Payment satisfies.

An allocation must reference the Payment that created it.

## Allocation Types

For incoming Party/Company payments:

- Specific Bill
- Specific Trip
- Bulk/FIFO

The allocation record must preserve:

- Payment ID
- Allocation Type
- Allocated Amount
- Trip reference where applicable
- Bill reference where applicable
- Allocation Date
- FIFO information where applicable

---

# 19. Specific Bill Allocation

A Company Payment can be allocated directly to a specific Bill.

The system must:

1. Identify the selected Bill.
2. Validate the amount.
3. Apply the payment to the Bill.
4. Update related outstanding amounts.
5. Recalculate Bill status.
6. Recalculate related Trip settlement where applicable.
7. Preserve the Payment and Allocation records.

If the payment is insufficient to fully account for the Bill, the Bill remains unpaid.

There is no `Partially Paid` Bill status.

The Bill remains:

- Generated, or
- Submitted

until its full amount is accounted for.

---

# 20. Specific Trip Allocation

An incoming payment can be allocated directly to a specific Trip.

The system must:

- Validate that the Trip has an applicable receivable.
- Apply the amount.
- Update the Trip's accounted amount.
- Recalculate outstanding receivable.
- Recalculate settlement.
- Preserve the allocation history.

---

# 21. Bulk / FIFO Allocation

A bulk incoming payment may be allocated against multiple outstanding Party/Company receivables.

FIFO must determine the allocation order.

The system must apply the payment to eligible outstanding receivables according to the defined FIFO sequence.

If the payment exceeds all applicable outstanding receivables:

`Excess = Payment Amount - Total Eligible Outstanding`

The excess becomes Party/Company Credit.

---

# 22. FIFO Ownership

Only the Owner can:

- Create FIFO bulk/unallocated payment allocations.
- Reallocate completed payment allocations.
- Change the allocation of a completed payment.

Staff cannot perform these actions.

CA has view-only access.

---

# 23. Full Allocation Rule

A completed Payment cannot remain partially allocated.

The entire payment amount must be accounted for.

For Party/Company incoming payments:

`Payment Amount = Applied Receivable + Generated Credit`

Therefore:

- Specific allocation must account for the full amount.
- FIFO allocation must account for the full amount.
- Any excess becomes credit.

For standalone Other Business Payments and Other Business Receipts, the entire amount belongs to their selected category.

---

# 24. Party / Company Credit

Credit is created when an incoming Party/Company payment exceeds the receivable amount that can be applied.

Credit applies to:

- Companies.
- Market Parties.

Credit does not apply to:

- Vehicle Owners.

## Credit Information

The system should preserve:

- Source Payment.
- Generated amount.
- Utilized amount.
- Remaining amount.
- Generation date.
- Utilization history.

---

# 25. Credit Utilization

Party/Company credit can be used against future receivables.

The system must preserve the relationship between:

- Original Payment.
- Credit generated.
- Credit utilization.
- Future receivable.

Credit utilization must not create a duplicate Payment.

It is an allocation of previously received money.

---

# 26. Vehicle Owner Overpayment

Vehicle Owner payments do not create Vehicle Owner credit.

The system must not carry an excess Vehicle Owner payment forward as a reusable credit balance.

Any such situation must be handled according to the authorized financial correction workflow.

Do not automatically introduce a Vehicle Owner credit ledger.

---

# 27. Payment Reallocation

The Owner can reallocate an existing Payment.

Reallocation requires:

- Confirmation/warning.
- New allocation.
- Recalculation of affected financial records.
- Audit entry.

The same Payment ID is retained.

The system must not create a duplicate Payment merely because its allocation changes.

---

# 28. Partial Redistribution

An existing payment may be redistributed across multiple valid allocations.

However, after the reallocation is completed:

`Total Allocated Amount + Credit = Payment Amount`

The Payment cannot remain partially unaccounted.

The allocation system must always reach a complete state.

---

# 29. Payment Reversal

Only the Owner can reverse a Payment.

A Payment is never permanently deleted.

Instead, the Owner performs a reversal.

Required:

- Reversal confirmation.
- Mandatory reversal reason.
- Reversed By.
- Reversal Date/Time.
- Audit entry.

---

# 30. Effects of Payment Reversal

When a Payment is reversed:

1. Payment status becomes `Reversed`.
2. Existing allocations are reversed.
3. Related outstanding amounts are recalculated.
4. Related Party/Company credit is recalculated.
5. Trip settlement is recalculated.
6. Bill payment status is recalculated.
7. Payment-based financial reports are recalculated.
8. P&L is recalculated.
9. Audit history records the reversal.

The original Payment record remains permanently available for historical reference.

---

# 31. Payment Editing

Authorized users may edit permitted Payment information.

Editing must:

- Keep the same Payment ID.
- Preserve the previous values.
- Store the new values.
- Record who made the edit.
- Record when the edit occurred.
- Create an audit record.
- Recalculate affected financial information where required.

Payment history must never silently overwrite important historical changes.

---

# 32. Payment Audit History

Payment actions must maintain a chronological audit history.

Important actions include:

- Payment creation.
- Payment editing.
- Allocation creation.
- Allocation modification.
- FIFO allocation.
- Reallocation.
- Payment reversal.
- Status changes.

Where applicable, audit history must preserve:

- Previous value.
- New value.
- User.
- Role.
- Date/Time.
- Reason.

---

# 33. Trip Synchronization

Trip financial information must synchronize with the Payment Module.

Examples:

### Incoming Party Payment

Payment Module records:

- Payment.
- Allocation.

Trip reflects:

- Amount Accounted/Received.
- Outstanding.
- Settlement status.

### Vehicle Owner Payment

Payment Module records:

- Outgoing Payment.
- Allocation.

Trip reflects:

- Amount Paid.
- Outstanding Payable.
- Settlement status.

The Trip must reference the actual Payment record rather than duplicating it.

---

# 34. Bill Synchronization

Bills must synchronize with actual Payment allocations.

When a payment is allocated to a Bill:

- Bill outstanding is recalculated.
- If the full bill amount is accounted for, Bill becomes `Paid`.
- If the payment is insufficient, Bill remains `Generated` or `Submitted`.
- If the payment is reversed, Bill status is recalculated.

Bill status must never be manually changed to `Paid` independently of Payment records.

---

# 35. Settlement Synchronization

Trip settlement must be derived from the applicable financial sides.

## Market Trip

Trip becomes Settled only when:

- Party-side financial obligation is fully accounted for.
- Vehicle Owner payable is fully accounted for.

## Own Fleet Trip

Trip becomes Settled when:

- Party-side receivable is fully accounted for.

Own Fleet expenses may be added after settlement.

Adding an Own Fleet expense after settlement:

- Recalculates Trip P&L.
- Does not change the Trip back from Settled.
- Does not create a Vehicle Owner payable.

---

# 36. Payment-Based Business P&L

The Payment Module is the authority for overall business P&L.

Formula:

`Business P&L = Active Incoming Payments - Active Outgoing Payments`

## Incoming

Includes:

- Company Payments.
- Market Party Payments.
- Other Business Receipts.

## Outgoing

Includes:

- Vehicle Owner Payments.
- Own Fleet Expenses.
- Other Business Payments.

Reversed payments are excluded.

---

# 37. Payment Summary

The Payment Module must support a summary containing:

- Total Incoming.
- Total Outgoing.
- Net P&L.
- Payment Count.
- Reversed Payment Count.

Incoming breakdown:

- Company Payments.
- Market Party Payments.
- Other Business Receipts.

Outgoing breakdown:

- Vehicle Owner Payments.
- Own Fleet Expenses.
- Other Business Payments.

---

# 38. Payment Filters

Payment reports and lists must support:

- Financial Year.
- Date range.
- Incoming/Outgoing.
- Entity.
- Category.
- Payment Mode.
- Status.
- Trip.
- Bill.

Filters may be combined.

---

# 39. Payment List

The Payment List must display only the approved fields:

1. Payment ID
2. Date
3. Type
4. Entity
5. Category
6. Amount
7. Mode
8. Status

The list must support:

- Search.
- Combined filters.
- Desktop Table/Card toggle.
- Mobile compact cards.

---

# 40. Payment Details

Payment Details must include:

## Basic Information

- Payment ID.
- Date.
- Type.
- Entity.
- Category.
- Amount.
- Mode.
- Status.

## User Information

- Created By.
- Created Date/Time.
- Last Edited By.
- Last Edited Date/Time.

## Relationships

- Trip.
- Bill.
- Party/Company.
- Vehicle Owner.
- Own Fleet.
- Other Business Category.

## Allocation

- Allocated Amount.
- Allocation Type.
- Trip/Bill.
- Allocation Date.
- Remaining Unallocated Amount.
- FIFO Details.
- Credit Generated.

A completed payment should normally have zero remaining unallocated amount.

## Additional Information

- Attachment.
- Audit History.
- Reversal Details.

---

# 41. Payment Attachments

Payments may contain an optional attachment where applicable.

The attachment must remain associated with the Payment record.

The attachment does not replace the structured Payment information.

---

# 42. Financial-Year Records

Payment records must be associated with their applicable Financial Year.

Financial-year reporting must support:

- Total Incoming.
- Total Outgoing.
- Net P&L.
- Payment Count.
- Category totals.
- Monthly breakdown.
- Underlying Payments.
- Reversed Payment History.
- Export.

Historical Financial Years must remain accessible.

---

# 43. Payment-Based TDS Reporting

TDS reporting may connect:

- Party/Company.
- Trip.
- TDS Amount.
- Related Payments.
- Financial Year.
- Relevant Date.
- TDS status/position.

TDS itself remains a financial adjustment rather than a Payment.

---

# 44. Payment Corrections

The system must use controlled correction mechanisms.

### Payment Information Error

Use Payment Edit.

### Incorrect Allocation

Use Payment Reallocation.

### Invalid/Cancelled Payment

Use Payment Reversal.

### Never

Do not permanently delete a completed Payment.

Do not create a replacement Payment merely to hide an incorrect historical Payment.

---

# 45. Authorization

## Owner

Can:

- Create payments.
- Edit payments.
- Reverse payments.
- Reallocate payments.
- Create FIFO bulk payments.
- View all payment records.
- View all payment audit history.

## Staff

Can:

- Create payments.
- Edit payments.
- View payments.
- View allocations.
- View financial results according to access.

Staff cannot:

- Reverse payments.
- Reallocate completed payments.
- Create FIFO bulk/unallocated payments.

## CA

Can:

- View payments.
- View allocations.
- View audit history.
- Use payment information for financial/tax workflows.

CA cannot modify payments.

---

# 46. Non-Negotiable Payment Rules

1. Payment Module is the only authoritative source of actual money movement.
2. Payments are never permanently deleted.
3. Reversal is used instead of deletion.
4. Only Owner can reverse a Payment.
5. Only Owner can reallocate a completed Payment.
6. Only Owner can create FIFO bulk/unallocated payment allocations.
7. Completed Payments cannot remain partially allocated.
8. Company and Market Party excess payments become credit.
9. Vehicle Owner payments do not create credit.
10. Reversed Payments do not contribute to financial totals.
11. Payment reversal must recalculate affected records.
12. Payment allocation must be auditable.
13. Payment editing must preserve historical audit information.
14. Bill `Paid` status must be derived from active Payment allocations.
15. Trip settlement must react to Payment changes.
16. Overall business P&L must be derived from active Payment records.
17. Trip financial data must not become a duplicate payment ledger.
18. Payment-based Financial Reports must use Payment Module records as their source.
